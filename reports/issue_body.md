### 6. [Biomechanical stiffening effect in ultra-high-fluence extracorporeal crosslinked corneal allogenic intrastromal ring segments](https://doi.org/10.1097/j.jcrs.0000000000001981)

**Authors:** M. Enes Aydemir, Léonard Kollros, Mark Hillen, Craig Boote, Keith M. Meek, Sally Hayes, Emilio A. Torres-Netto, Shady T. Awwad, Michalina Depczyńska, Nikki Hafezi, Farhad Hafezi  
**Venue/category:** Journal of Cataract & Refractive Surgery  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**DOI:** `10.1097/j.jcrs.0000000000001981`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

Purpose: To compare the biomechanical stiffening effects of a new ultra-high-fluence corneal crosslinking (CXL) protocol with conventional high-fluence CXL for extracorporeal corneal allogenic intrastromal ring segment optimization (ECO-CAIRS). Setting: ELZA Institute, Zurich, Switzerland. Design: Experimental, randomized, controlled, ex vivo laboratory study using porcine corneas. No masking was applied. Methods: 84 porcine corneas were divided into 4 groups: Group A (control), Group B (10 J/cm 2 ), Group C (30 J/cm 2 ), and Group D (60 J/cm 2 ). After epithelial debridement, Groups B, C, and D were treated with 0.1% hypo-osmolar riboflavin. UV-A irradiation was delivered at fluences of 10 J/cm 2 (18 mW/cm 2 , 9:15 min) for Group B, 30 J/cm 2 (30 mW/cm 2 , 16:40 min) for Group C, and 60 J/cm 2 (30 mW/cm 2 , 33:18 min) for Group D. Corneal biomechanical properties were assessed by stress‑strain extensiometry. Results: Mean elastic modulus (5% strain) values were as follows: Group A (1.39 ± 0.49 N/mm), Group B (1.83 ± 0.48 N/mm), Group C (2.25 ± 0.83 N/mm), and Group D (2.31 ± 0.88 N/mm). Ultra-high-fluence groups (C and D) showed significantly greater stiffening than Group B ( P = .041 and P = .015), with no significant difference between Groups C and D ( P = 1.00). Group B exhibited significantly greater stiffening than Group A ( P = .023). Conclusions: Ultra-high-fluence CXL enhances biomechanical stiffening compared with conventional high-fluence CXL, supporting its use in ECO-CAIRS. In this extracorporeal context, endothelial safety and haze are irrelevant, while increased stiffness facilitates intrastromal ring insertion and reduces corneal swelling.

### 9. [Digital Light Processing of Thermoplastic Poly(methyl methacrylate) Photoresin Combined With Solvent Vapor Smoothing](https://doi.org/10.1002/adem.71348)

**Authors:** Rozhin Heydarpour, Yuanyuan Li, Sebastian Kluck, Afnan Quazzie‐Hauser, Bastian E. Rapp, Frederik Kotz‐Helmer  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Engineering Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-25  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adem.71348`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

3D printing of complex geometries remains limited by the trade‐off between printing resolution and surface quality: fused deposition modeling allows efficient solvent‐based surface smoothing but offers limited resolution, whereas vat‐photopolymerization provides high resolution but typically uses crosslinked thermosets that restrict solvent‐induced surface reflow. Here, we present a poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA)‐based photoresin, formulated from polymeric PMMA, methyl methacrylate, and butyl methacrylate, that enables high‐resolution digital light processing (DLP) while retaining thermoplastic characteristics. A wax additive suppressed rapid monomer evaporation, enabling stable printing on a benchtop DLP system. Incorporating 15 wt% butyl methacrylate prevented visible solvent‐induced cracking over 14 days, and a 50:50 (v/v) acetone:2‐propanol mixture gave effective smoothing after 120 min. This approach reduced the staircase‐induced surface roughness from 2.33 µm to 129 nm (86 × 86 µm 2 evaluation area), while local measurements on treated microlenses (10 × 10 µm 2 ) yielded S q = 12.8 ± 4.4 nm. The optical transmittance of 2 mm thick samples increased from approximately 30% to 80% in the visible range. The presented strategy combines high‐resolution vat‐polymerization with effective surface smoothing and provides a viable route towards smooth and optically transparent PMMA components, particularly for illumination optics and applications requiring complex geometries.
