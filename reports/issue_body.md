### 1. [Efficient white electroluminescence based on a single multi-resonance TADF emitter with dual emission feature](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwag687)

**Authors:** Yufang Nie, Baoyan Liang, Chao Jiang, Chi Cao, Xuming Zhuang, Zhiqiang Li, Yue Wang  
**Venue/category:** National Science Review  
**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**DOI:** `10.1093/nsr/nwag687`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 19  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled, organic light-emitting diode  

**Abstract**

Abstract Pure organic white organic light-emitting diodes (WOLEDs) based on a single dopant offer distinct advantages over traditional WOLEDs with RGB emitters, especially in terms of simple fabrication process and low cost. However, the maximum external quantum efficiency (EQEmax) of the reported pure organic single-dopant WOLED devices remains below 15%, which could be attributed to insufficient exciton utilization. Herein, a multi-resonance thermally activated delayed fluorescence (MR-TADF) molecule (Th-DBN) with extended π-conjugation is reported, along with highly efficient excimer emission and dual TADF features. The single-dopant WOLEDs based on Th-DBN deliver outstanding performance, achieving a record EQEmax of 27.1%, Commission Internationale de l’Eclairage (CIE) coordinates of (0.35, 0.50), and a significant operational lifetime (LT80) of 398 h at 1000 cd m−2, which are among the best reported results of pure organic single-dopant WOLEDs. This work not only provides the first example of a single-dopant WOLED based on an MR-TADF molecule, but also paves the way to designing pure organic WOLEDs with a single dopant and efficient exciton utilization.

### 9. [Methacrylate-Functionalized BaTiO3 for High-Luminance Stretchable Alternating-Current Electroluminescent Devices](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03859)

**Authors:** Eunji Jung, Shakti Singh, Jinhwan Yoon  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Polymer Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsapm.6c03859`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** stretchable  

**Abstract**

Abstract Blending a high-permittivity ceramic such as barium titanate (BTO) into the emission layer is an established route to brighter alternating-current electroluminescent (ACEL) devices, but the hydrophilic ceramic aggregates in silicone elastomers and caps the loading attainable in a thin stretchable layer. BTO nanoparticles were modified with 3-(trimethoxysilyl)propyl methacrylate through an acid-catalyzed sol–gel reaction that leaves a thin methacrylate shell on the particle surface. The modified filler (m-BTO) dispersed uniformly in the elastomer and entered blade-coated emission layers at loadings beyond the reach of the bare particles. The m-BTO composites increased the dielectric constant of the emission layer more efficiently than the BTO composites while retaining or improving the stretchability. The resulting ACEL devices were about 30% brighter than the BTO devices at equal loading. The accessible 25 wt % loading increased the peak luminance to 515 cd/m2 at 400 V and 30 kHz, 2.7 times that of the phosphor-only device, with the blue emission color unchanged. The devices operated under twisting, folding, and stretching and sustained emission to 500% strain.

### 10. [A Multi‐Crosslinkable Methacrylated Alginate‐GelMa Bioink for Drop‐on‐Demand Bioprinting](https://doi.org/10.1002/mabi.70278)

**Authors:** Tommi Bui, Emine Karakaya, Jessica Faber, Jonas Röder, Jörg Teßmar, Julian Bauer, Oliver Friedrich, Silvia Budday, Aldo R. Boccaccini, Rainer Detsch  
**Venue/category:** Macromolecular Bioscience  
**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**DOI:** `10.1002/mabi.70278`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT To advance biofabrication, this work presents the development of a multi‐crosslinkable bioink based on methacrylated alginate di‐aldehyde (ADAMa) combined with gelatine methacryloyl (GelMa). ADAMa was synthesized using methacrylic anhydride under specific conditions, and successful functionalization as well as changes in chain length were confirmed by NMR (Nuclear Magnetic Resonance), ATR‐FTIR (Attenuated Total Reflectance—Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy), and GPC (Gel Permeation Chromatography) analyses. The resulting ADAMa–GelMa bioink can be crosslinked via several independent mechanisms, including UV‐initiated photopolymerization, enzymatic crosslinking, Schiff‐base formation, and ionic gelation with calcium ions. Our study confirms an increase in the compressive modulus with increasing GelMa content, ranging from 0.3 to 30 kPa, and in vitro biocompatibility for ADAMa–GelMa bioink with enhanced cell–material interaction for a 25:75 ADAMa–GelMa ratio. Furthermore, Drop‐on‐Demand (DoD) bioprinting performance was evaluated using filament fusion test and grid structure test analyses, along with structural fidelity assessment of free‐standing pillar arrays, including metrics such as pillar height and spacing. Multiphoton microscopy further revealed homogeneous cell distribution within printed pillars. Overall, this system provides tuneable mechanical properties, high cell viability, and strong structural fidelity, making it a promising candidate for the fabrication of complex, cell‐laden constructs in advanced biofabrication applications.
