**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15011313/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 14  
**Matched:** tadf, oled, organic light-emitting diode  

**Abstract**

2 Abstract Despite their potential to deliver high-efficiency organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs), those using thermally activated delayed fluorescent (TADF) emitters often suffer from a large drop in efficiency at high luminance. This effect is known as efficiency roll-off and is a longstanding issue for these devices, which has limited their commercial deployment. We have discovered that the degree of overlap between the photoluminescence (PL) and absorption spectra drives singlet exciton diffusion and is a strong predictor of the magnitude of efficiency roll-off in TADF OLEDs. This effect is especially important in OLEDs made with multiresonant (MR)-TADF materials given their intense low-energy short-range charge transfer absorption bands. MR-TADF OLEDs are very appealing due to their narrow emission spectra; however, their small Stokes shift leads to large spectral overlap and contributes to these devices showing severe efficiency roll-off. Developing TADF materials that have a larger Stokes shift whilst maintaining their bright, narrow emission would help minimize efficiency roll-off while securing high internal quantum efficiencies, features particularly relevant for displays.

### 27. [Liquid metal interfaces for component mounting on kirigami stretchable substrates](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41528-026-00662-1)

**Authors:** Takashi Sato, Wataru Iwasaki, Eiji Iwase  
**Venue/category:** npj Flexible Electronics  
**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41528-026-00662-1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** stretchable  

**Abstract**

Abstract This study demonstrates liquid metal as an electrical interface material (LM-EIM) for mounting rigid electronic components on kirigami-based stretchable substrates. Kirigami structures impart high stretch tolerance to sheets made of materials with low stretchability through local bending and twisting. However, this advantage can be lost when rigid components are mounted using rigid EIMs, such as solder or conductive adhesive, because they mechanically constrain kirigami deformation and cause fracture at low strains. The LM-EIM itself exhibited high stretch tolerance, 24 and 35 times those of solder and adhesive, respectively. This highly deformable LM-EIM reduced constraints on kirigami deformation and suppressed fracture. Mounting with solder and adhesive reduced the substrate stretch tolerance to 32% and 36% of that without components, respectively. In contrast, mounting with the LM-EIM did not reduce the substrate stretch tolerance. The substrate fracture strain reached 3.99, corresponding to 1.1 times that without components. Finally, a resistor array maintained electrical connectivity up to a device strain of 2.0, while an LED array operated stably up to 1.5. This approach allows rigid, high-performance components to be integrated onto highly stretchable kirigami sheets without sacrificing their stretchability, enabling the development of high-performance, multifunctional stretchable electronics.

### 33. [Efficient and Stable Red QLEDs via 4-TBP-Doped Hole Transport Layer Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c16350)

**Authors:** Renjie Xiang, Tianshuo Zhang, Zurong Huang, Xinyu Wang, Ziming Zhou, Dexia Zhou, Zhaobing Tang, Siqi Jia, Jie Lin, Lishuang Wang, Xiao Wei Sun

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsami.6c16350`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 6  
**Matched:** solution-processed, emissive layer  

**Abstract**

Abstract The insufficient charge carrier mobility in the hole transport layer (HTL) and the energy level mismatch with the emissive layer have long been critical factors limiting the electroluminescence performance and carrier balance of quantum dot light-emitting diodes (QLEDs). To address this issue, we employed 4-tert-butylpyridine (4-TBP) as a dopant in poly[(9,9-dioctylfluorenyl-2,7-diyl)-co-(4,4′-(N-(4-sec-butylphenyl)diphenylamine))] (TFB) as the HTL. By modulating the highest occupied molecular orbital energy level of TFB and enhancing the hole mobility, 4-TBP doping effectively reduces the hole injection barrier and suppresses interfacial charge accumulation. The optimized TFB:4-TBP HTL significantly improves hole injection efficiency in red QLEDs, leading to a decrease in turn-on voltage from 1.74 V to 1.65 V and an increase in external quantum efficiency (EQE) from 22.21 to 26.47%. Moreover, at an initial luminance of 1000 cd m–2, the QLED with the TFB:4-TBP HTL reaches a T95 operational lifetime of 11,708.67 h, which is 2.4 times longer than that of the undoped one. 1.22- and 1.37-fold improvements in EQEmax are obtained for green and blue QLEDs, respectively. This strategy demonstrates that 4-TBP doping, combined with interface engineering, provides an effective method to achieve efficient and stable hole transport, offering new insights for carrier balance control in solution-processed QLEDs.
