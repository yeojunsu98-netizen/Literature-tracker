### 1. [Beyond single numbers: how to choose host materials for deep-blue TADF OLEDs](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011505/v1)

**Authors:** Manmohan Dash, M. N. Satyanarayan  
**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15011505/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 17  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled  

**Abstract**

Deep-blue thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) OLEDs stand or fall on their host, yet hosts are still chosen from a handful of single numbers: triplet energy, singlet–triplet gap, frontier levels, glass transition and carrier mobility. We compiled these descriptors for sixteen hosts and cohost components from six chemical families, tracing the source, method and condition behind every value, and find that most are protocol-dependent observables rather than material constants: published triplet energies of one molecule differ by up to 0.12 eV, and five analyses of the same devices place one host’s electron-to-hole mobility ratio anywhere between 0.77 and 3.1. A one-protocol calculation of 50 molecules shows that computed-gap rankings depend on functional choice and require validation against measurement. We review how exciplex-forming, mixed and sensitised hosts share the host’s tasks and what limits operational stability, and propose a minimum reporting standard and a roadmap toward protocol-locked, multi-descriptor host selection.

### 2. [Colour-Stable When Heated, Colour-Shifting When Excited: Deep-Blue TADF Hosts Need Vector, Not Scalar, Screening](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011501/v1)

**Authors:** Manmohan Dash, M. N. Satyanarayan  
**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15011501/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf  

**Abstract**

Deep-blue thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials are screened by a single figure of meritquantum yield, singlet–triplet gap, colour shift or activation energy—on the assumption that a material robust under one stress is robust under others. Here we show that this assumption fails. For three related phosphineoxide hosts, the host whose emission colour moves least on heating is the one whose colour moves most when the excitation wavelength is changed: an 8-to 17-fold reversal, read directly from instrument chromaticity coordinates. Solvent response, spectral dimensionality, quantum yield and delayed-fluorescence kinetics each add a different ordering, and no host is best on every axis. A thermal screen would therefore advance precisely the most excitationsensitive material. We replace the stability score with a four-axis descriptor vector—environment, excitation, temperature and time—that keeps each vulnerability visible and lets the intended application decide which tradeoff is acceptable, before a material is committed to devices.

### 5. [The tail of a flash lamp limits which delayed-fluorescence kinetics can be measured](https://doi.org/10.1364/opticaopen.34875729.v1)

**Authors:** Manmohan Dash, Satyanarayan Nagarajan  
**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**DOI:** `10.1364/opticaopen.34875729.v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 11  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf  

**Abstract**

Rate constants and activation energies of reverse intersystem crossing, used to rank thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) emitters, are often extracted from decays excited by xenon flash lamps. We show that the temporal tail of the excitation, not its nominal width, decides which kinetics such measurements can resolve. In three deep-blue host materials, the dominant "fast delayed" component lies inside the excitation window and follows the excitation profile. Passed through reconstructed excitation profiles, 86 reported literature lifetimes used as simulation inputs show that standard tail fits err by more than a factor of two in 41% of cases for a microsecond-tailed lamp, against 6% for a clean 2-µs pulse, and that activation energies are compressed. Reconvolution with the generating profile largely removes the simulated bias; a measurability map identifies conditions requiring an independently recorded excitation profile.

### 25. [A Diffusion‐Based Evaluation of Transport‐Effective Network Characteristics in Fully PEG ‐Based Tetra/Linear‐ PEG Hydrogels](https://doi.org/10.1002/app.71732)

**Authors:** Sangming Xu, Ryoichi Ohta, Ruying Wang, Noritada Kaji, Masaya Miyazaki, Ruchi Gupta, Kazuma Mawatari  
**Venue/category:** Journal of Applied Polymer Science  
**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**DOI:** `10.1002/app.71732`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Tetra‐arm poly(ethylene glycol) (tetra‐PEG) hydrogels are well‐defined PEG‐based polymer networks with excellent optical transparency; however, their relatively restricted transport pathways can limit the diffusion of macromolecules. In this work, we present a diffusion‐based strategy to evaluate transport‐effective characteristics of PEG hydrogels using macromolecular diffusion measurements. Hydrogels were prepared using 20 kDa four‐arm PEG–succinimidyl ester crosslinked with either four‐arm PEG–amine (TP) or bifunctional linear PEG diamine (TP + LP), forming 4 × 4 and 2 × 4 PEG network architectures, respectively. Time‐resolved diffusion experiments were performed using 70 kDa fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)–dextran as a model macromolecule, and effective diffusion coefficients ( D eff ) were obtained from the release profiles. An obstruction‐based diffusion model was subsequently applied to estimate diffusion‐effective characteristic length scales associated with macromolecular transport. The TP + LP formulation exhibited enhanced FITC–dextran transport, with a mean effective diffusion coefficient approximately 3.4‐fold higher than that of TP. The corresponding model‐derived diffusion‐effective characteristic lengths were 8.7 ± 0.9 nm for TP and 24 ± 7 nm for TP + LP. These results demonstrate diffusion measurements can provide a comparative evaluation of transport accessibility in PEG hydrogel formulations. The present study provides a proof‐of‐concept approach for relating PEG network formulation to macromolecular transport behavior.
