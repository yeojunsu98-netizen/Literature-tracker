### 1. [Transport Layer-Driven Optimization of Color and Efficiency in TADF OLEDs for Large-Area Applications](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33933v1)

**Authors:** Manish Kumar, Jawid Nikan, Luiz Pereira  
**Venue/category:** physics.optics  
**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**arXiv:** `2611.33933`  
**Source(s):** arXiv  
**Keyword score:** 22  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled, organic light-emitting diode, emissive layer  

**Abstract**

Balancing structural simplicity with high efficiency is crucial for practical applications of organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs), especially in large-area lighting devices. Optimizing the charge-transport layers in multilayer OLEDs controls the recombination zone within the emissive layer, thereby enhancing the device's performance. Herein, we experimentally and computationally investigate how the thicknesses of the electron and hole transport layers affect the performance of orange-red thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) OLEDs. Using a TADF emitter, 2-[4-(diphenylamino)phenyl]-10,10-dioxide-9H-thioxanthen-9-one (TXO-TPA), at 10 wt\% concentration in 4,4-Bis(N-carbazolyl)-1,1-biphenyl (CBP) host, we achieve a maximum external quantum efficiency ($η_{EQE}$) of 20.86\% and a peak luminance of 32,800 cd m$^{-2}$. Using a combination of drift-diffusion and optical simulations, we reveal how variations in hole- and electron-transport-layer thicknesses modulate the charge-carrier balance and shift the recombination zone within the emissive layer. This tuning not only optimizes exciton formation but also enables color tuning from green to red without altering the emissive material. Our findings highlight the critical role of transport layer engineering in achieving efficient, color-controlled OLEDs with simplified architectures. Furthermore, we successfully demonstrate a large-area OLED device (~20 cm$^{2}$), achieving an impressive $η_{EQE}$ of 17\%, underscoring the scalability and practicality of our approach for general lighting applications.

### 2. [Design Principles and Recent Advances of Organic Multiphoton‐Active TADF Materials: From Two‐Photon to Three‐Photon Excitation](https://doi.org/10.1002/cptc.70336)

**Authors:** Youhei Chitose, Mageswari Gomathi Vinayakam, Tzu‐Chau Lin, Ja‐Hon Lin, Chihaya Adachi  
**Venue/category:** ChemPhotoChem  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.1002/cptc.70336`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 16  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled  

**Abstract**

Multiphoton‐active thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials have emerged as a distinctive class of organic emitters that integrate nonlinear excitation with efficient triplet harvesting. This review highlights recent progress in TADF systems exhibiting two‐photon absorption (2PA) and the emergence of three‐photon absorption (3PA), collectively termed multiphoton absorption (MPA). Highly emissive MPA‐TADF materials capable of generating delayed fluorescence under near‐infrared (NIR) excitation have expanded applications to bioimaging and photodynamic therapy (PDT). Although such systems require sophisticated molecular design, precise control of the balance between locally excited (LE) and charge‐transfer (CT) states has proven crucial for enhancing MPA cross‐sections, reverse intersystem crossing (RISC), and radiative decay processes. These design strategies have also been translated into optoelectronic applications, leading to improved organic light‐emitting diode (OLED) performance. Furthermore, the realization of intrinsic 3PA in purely organic TADF emitters represents a significant advance in the field, enabling NIR‐II excitation while maintaining high photoluminescence quantum yields (PLQYs) and efficient TADF behavior. Collectively, these advances position MPA‐TADF materials as versatile platforms bridging photonics, biomedical applications, and optoelectronics. We conclude by outlining key challenges and future directions for MPA‐TADF materials.

### 3. [Intrinsic High-κ Elastic Dielectrics from Dual-Fluorinated Polyurethane Urea Enable Low-Voltage Stretchable Organic Transistors](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c19431)

**Authors:** Jiabing Mei, Wei-Chen Gao, Defeng Cui, Zhaohan Ding, Junmei Hu, Yu-Dong Zhao, Ben Fan, Xiang Sun, Jing Qiao, Ying-Shi Guan  
**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsami.6c19431`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 14  
**Matched:** stretchable, intrinsically stretchable  

**Abstract**

Abstract Low-voltage operation in intrinsically stretchable transistors fundamentally relies on gate dielectrics that reconcile intrinsic stretchability with a high dielectric constant (high-κ). However, reconciling enhanced dielectric performance with robust mechanical resilience remains a persistent challenge. Here, we report a molecular engineering strategy to synthesize a series of functionalized polyurethane urea (PUU) by rationally incorporating distinct functional groups into the hard-segment domains. Among the synthesized variants, the difluorinated analogue (2F-PUU) achieves an optimal trade-off, combining a high κ of 7.06 at 1 kHz, exceptional stretchability up to 1623%, strain-insensitive dielectric behavior, solvent reprocessability, and an ultralow surface roughness of 0.273 nm. Stretchable organic transistors employing 2F-PUU as the gate dielectric operate at a low voltage of –10 V and sustain stable electrical performance under 50% tensile strain. Moreover, a 5 × 5 transistor array based on 2F-PUU delivers a 100% fabrication yield, with a peak mobility of 0.51 cm2 V–1 s–1 and a maximum on/off current ratio exceeding 104. Collectively, this work establishes a viable molecular design roadmap for intrinsically stretchable high-κ dielectrics, paving the way toward low-power, mechanically durable stretchable organic electronics.

### 7. [Bridge‐Controlled Chromophore Decoupling in Dual‐Chromophore TADF Emitters](https://doi.org/10.1002/adom.72010)

**Authors:** Emma V. Puttock, Daniel Crane, Rasmus Ringström, Fabian Schönle, Kamilė Bareikaitė, Sebastian Roth, Andrew P. Monkman, Andrew Danos, Karl Börjesson, Stefan Bräse  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Optical Materials  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adom.72010`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** tadf  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Intramolecular hyperfluorescent emitters incorporate both TADF donor and fluorescent acceptor moieties within a single covalent framework, potentially surpassing random mixing in conventional hyperfluorescent blends. However, understanding and controlling excited‐state communication between linked chromophores remains a key challenge. Here we investigate model systems in which two identical TADF emitters are linked. In contrast to active energy transfer systems, the use of identical chromophores allows changes in their spectra and kinetics to directly reveal the effectiveness of different bridging groups in maintaining electronic isolation. Aided by computational methods, we show that highly twisted or non‐conjugated bridges enforce chromophore isolation and preserve the intrinsic photophysical characteristics of the parent monomer. In contrast, partially conjugated bridges promote excited‐state delocalization, resulting in bathochromically shifted emission and reduced TADF performance. Comparison with monomer‐bridge reference compounds confirms that the observed changes arise from modification of the emissive cores rather than emergent interchromophore effects. Of the bridges investigated, only adamantyl‐ or tetramethyl‐substituted bridges preserve the degree of chromophore isolation required for the design of future intramolecular hyperfluorescent emitters.
