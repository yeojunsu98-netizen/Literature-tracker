### 12. [Properties and Interfacial Stabilization of a Crosslinked Hydroxypropyl Guar Aqueous Gel in a CO2-Rich Mixed-Phase Fracturing Fluid](https://doi.org/10.3390/gels12111003)

**Authors:** Guojun Li, Meilong Fu, Tengfei Chen, Yuhao Zhu  
**Venue/category:** Gels  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.3390/gels12111003`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

A crosslinked hydroxypropyl guar (HPG) aqueous gel was investigated as the continuous, structure-forming phase of a CO2-rich mixed-phase fracturing fluid for unconventional gas reservoirs. The CO2 fraction was first screened by fluid-loss tests, after which the 30%, 40%, and 50% formulations were compared through proppant-settling tests. Although the 30% formulation exhibited the lowest cumulative fluid loss, the 40% formulation showed the lowest proppant-settling velocity and was selected for subsequent evaluation of phase behavior, gel-phase rheology, flow resistance, interfacial stabilization, and true-triaxial fracturing performance. Stable mixed-phase structures were maintained at 20–80 °C and 10–20 MPa, while bubble characteristics, rheology, and pipe-flow resistance showed a distinct response near 40 °C. Comparison with previously reported AM/AA–CO2 visualization showed that different water-soluble polymers generated different structures after CO2 mixing. Newly measured surface-tension and viscoelastic data, together with molecular dynamics simulations, indicated that bulk gel rheology alone could not account for this difference. HPG exhibited pronounced enrichment near the CO2–water interfaces, whereas AM/AA remained predominantly within the aqueous phase. Combined with the component-removal and viscoelasticity results, these findings suggest that HPG-specific interfacial behavior contributes to the formation and organization of dispersed structures, while the crosslinked gel network provides additional bulk viscoelastic support.

### 14. [Crosslinked-grafted-blended polybenzimidazole alkaline anion exchange membranes for water electrolysis](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11324035/v1)

**Authors:** Luyang Ding, Limin Zhang, Xinji Yu, Yue Chen, Shuguo Qu  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11324035/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

Abstract Anion exchange membranes (AEMs), as the core material in anion exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE) for hydrogen production, play a pivotal role in enhancing electrolysis efficiency and extending the operational lifetime of electrolyzer. To improve the solubility and ionic conductivity of Poly[2,2′-(1,4-naphthalene)-5,5′-bibenzimidazole](NPBI)-based AEMs, methyl groups were grafted onto the pyrrole and pyridine nitrogen atoms of NPBI, and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) with different molar ratios was mixed with methyl grafted NPBI (MeNPBI) to prepare a grafted-blended PVA-MeNPBI AEM. By tuning the molar ratio of PVA-to-MeNPBI, the resulting membranes demonstrated significant improvements in ion exchange capacity, ionic conductivity, and elongation at break. Notably, the PVA-MeNPBI-4:1 membrane exhibited a hydroxide ion conductivity of 138.2 mS·cm⁻¹. The cationic crosslinker, 3-Hexyl spacers N,N-Dimethylpiperidinium cations (QDMP), was introduced into PVA-MeNPBI AEM via the Menshutkin reaction to produce QDMP-PVA-MeNPBI AEMs. The resulting membrane achieved a peak hydroxide ion conductivity of 150.3 mS·cm⁻¹, significantly surpassing that of the pristine MeNPBI membrane (91.3 mS·cm⁻¹) and 1.79 times higher than that of the commercial FAA-3 membrane (84.0 mS·cm⁻¹). This membrane exhibited a tensile strength of 57.1 MPa and an elongation at break of 63.4%, showing excellent mechanical stability. At 2.10V, the AEMWE with QDMP-PVA-MeNPBI membrane delivered a current density of 3.37 A·cm⁻², four times than that with the FAA-3 membrane (0.84 A·cm⁻²). After immersion in 1 M KOH at 80°C for 500 hours, the QDMP-PVA-MeNPBI membrane retained 92.4% of its initial conductivity. These results indicate that the QDMP-PVA-MeNPBI membrane exhibits excellent potential for application in AEMWE for hydrogen production.

### 15. [Comparative Evaluation of Dichromate Adsorption Parameters Using Colorimetry on Glutaraldehyde-Crosslinked Polyethylene Imine (PEI) and Polyaniline (PANI)](https://doi.org/10.3390/app162110916)

**Authors:** Thomas Tunidau, Ashika Chandra, Taitusi Taufa, Ashneel Ajay Singh, Francis S. Mani, Xun Pan, Mats R. Andersson, Desta Gedefaw  
**Venue/category:** Applied Sciences  
**Date:** 2026-11-03  
**DOI:** `10.3390/app162110916`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

Environmental pollution caused by hexavalent chromium [Cr(IV)] is a critical global concern due to its carcinogenic and acute toxicity. This study describes the synthesis of crosslinked polyethylene imine (PEI) and polyaniline (salt and base forms) and their effectiveness at removing dichromate ion (Cr2O72−) from aqueous solutions. The synthetized polymer adsorbents were comprehensively characterized using IR, TGA, and SEM-EDX methods. The adsorption processes were monitored via colorimetry and UV–Vis spectroscopy using the 1,5-diphenylcarbazide method under acidic conditions. Kinetic modeling revealed that the adsorption data fitted well to the pseudo-second-order model, confirming a chemisorption-controlled process. Equilibrium data followed the Langmuir isotherm at room temperature, predicting maximum adsorption capacities of 161 mg/g for PEI and exceeding 285 mg/g for the PANI salt. Thermodynamic parameters (ΔG°, ΔH°, and ΔS°) were all negative, confirming the spontaneous and exothermic nature of the adsorption process. In selective competitive studies, the PANI salt demonstrated exceptional robustness, maintaining a Cr(VI) removal efficiency of 93% to 99% in the concurrent presence of KH2PO4, NaNO3, and CuSO4 interferents, which is highly comparable to the control. Conversely, the removal efficiency of PEI was sensitive to the matrix, dropping to 52–53% in the presence of NaNO3 and CuSO4 while remaining unaffected (65%) by KH2PO4. Furthermore, regeneration studies using 5% HNO3 demonstrated stable reusability, with both polymers maintaining high adsorption efficiencies (88–99%) over four consecutive cycles. These findings highlight the significant potential of these accessible polymeric materials for functional integration into real-world industrial wastewater treatment facilities.

**Abstract**

Flexible hydrogel sensors hold great promise in wearable electronics, smart healthcare, and human–machine interfaces, yet their practical applications are often hindered by poor mechanical robustness, insufficient low-temperature tolerance, lack of self-healing ability, and inferior biocompatibility. Herein, we present a multifunctional ionic hydrogel composed of poly (acrylic acid), L-lysine, and aluminum ions (PAA-Lys-Al3+) for high-performance flexible strain sensors. Benefiting from its supramolecular network structure, the hydrogel exhibits efficient self-healing behavior and favorable antibacterial activity, as well as good biocompatibility. When assembled into a flexible ionic sensor, the PAA-Lys-Al3+ hydrogel delivers high sensitivity, fast response, and reliable signal output toward various mechanical deformations. This integrated combination of antifreeze-resistant, self-healing, tough, elastic, antibacterial, and biocompatible features makes the hydrogel highly competitive in flexible sensing devices operated under harsh environments and biomedical scenarios.

**Venue/category:** Angewandte Chemie International Edition

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Polymeric materials are susceptible to thermo‐oxidative degradation during melt processing and usage, which can be prevented using antioxidants. However, conventional small‐molecule antioxidants, such as phenolic antioxidants, are prone to migration, which compromises their long‐term effectiveness. To address this, for crystalline/semicrystalline polymers, we propose a systematic design strategy for macromolecular antioxidants based on the “3C principle” (Compatibility, cocrystallization, and crosslinking) to construct novel stabilizers with enhanced antioxidant performance and migration resistance. For noncrystalline polymers, macromolecular antioxidants satisfying only two of these criteria can also provide good antioxidant performance. Based on these design principles, three different types of macromolecular antioxidants were developed with structures tailored to the characteristics of commodity plastics, including polyethylene, polymethyl methacrylate, and polystyrene. This work provides a new approach for enhancing the service stability of polymeric materials and offers a broad molecular design strategy for functionalized polymer materials.

### 19. [Construction of a Thermoresponsive and Controllably Shrinkable Dual-Network Hydrogel Incorporating Copper-Doped Prussian Blue Nanozymes with In Vitro Antibacterial and Antioxidant Activities](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsanm.6c04076)

**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Nano Materials

**DOI:** `10.1021/acsanm.6c04076`  
**Source(s):** Crossref

**Abstract**

Abstract Infection-associated wounds are typically characterized by elevated bacterial burden, oxidative stress imbalance, and highly heterogeneous microenvironments, whereas conventional hydrogels remain limited in achieving rapid in situ gelation, stimuli-responsive deformation, and on-demand release of functional components. To address these challenges, this work presents a thermoresponsive, controllably shrinkable, copper-doped Prussian blue nanozyme-based dual-network hydrogel (SGCP@CuPB). In situ polymerized PNIPAM constitutes a thermosensitive covalent first network that enables rapid initial gelation and heat-triggered shrinkage, while an SA/GDL/CaCO3-based ionically crosslinked second network is subsequently established to progressively consolidate the network architecture and reinforce mechanical strength. Embedded CuPB nanozymes confer near-infrared (NIR) photothermal conversion, multi-enzyme-mimicking activity, and reactive oxygen species (ROS)-regulating capability. SGCP@CuPB-1 gels within 2 min, and its gelation time can be tuned from 2 to 10 min by adjusting the APS/TEMED dosage. Upon 808 nm NIR irradiation, SGCP@CuPB-1 undergoes controllable shrinkage, thereby promoting the outward migration and responsive release of CuPB. Cu doping modulates the local electronic environment of PB, enhancing its NIR absorption, photothermal conversion, and catalytic activity, which enables SGCP@CuPB to achieve a photothermal conversion efficiency of 37.0% while exhibiting SOD-like, CAT-like, and POD-like multi-enzyme-mimicking activities along with enhanced ROS-scavenging capacity. Upon NIR irradiation, SGCP@CuPB achieves antibacterial rates of 96.3%, 92.5%, and 86.8% against Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, respectively. Collectively, SGCP@CuPB integrates sequential dual-network construction, thermoresponsive and NIR-enhanced shrinkage, NIR-enhanced CuPB release, ROS regulation, and in vitro antibacterial function, providing a responsive hydrogel design for infection-associated wound microenvironments.
