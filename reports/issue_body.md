### 1. [Realizing Molecular Excited-State Regulation through Donor Molecular Engineering: The Intrinsic Relationship between TADF and HLCT Mechanisms](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.6c04193)

**Authors:** Chuanxin Liao, Jiaxu Bai, Bo Chen, Hongqiang Guo, Xianggao Li, Shirong Wang, Hongli Liu  
**Venue/category:** Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acs.iecr.6c04193`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 23  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled, organic light-emitting diode, solution-processed  

**Abstract**

Abstract For developing high-performance pure organic light-emitting diode (OLED) emitters, thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) and hybrid local and charge-transfer (HLCT) mechanisms are two pivotal strategies by harvesting triplet excitons via reverse intersystem crossing. The intrinsic relationship between the two in molecular design is revealed in this study: the switching between TADF and HLCT mechanisms can be achieved by adjusting the strength of the donor groups. In this work, five D-π-A-π-D type blue and green OLED emitters with acridine/phenoxazine/phenothiazine groups as donors were designed and synthesized. Compared to reported HLCT molecules adopting carbazole/diphenylamine groups as donors, the excited states of the molecules in this work were successfully regulated to TADF. The solution-processed blue/green OLED device based on FIP-DPAC/FIP-PXZ achieved the maximum external quantum efficiency (EQEmax) of 10.01%/17.45%. A strategy for regulating HLCT and TADF mechanisms of OLED emitters through donor molecular engineering was elucidated in this study.

### 2. [Fused Donor–Acceptor TADF Emitter Bearing Ancillary Donor: Toward High‐Performance OLED With Fast Reverse Intersystem Crossing](https://doi.org/10.1002/adom.71996)

**Authors:** Xiaoxiao Hu, Leyao Cai, Purui Zhang, Liang Deng, Zhiqiang Shi, Zhenhua Xu, Xusheng Xie, Ying Wang  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Optical Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.1002/adom.71996`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 17  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT The fast reverse intersystem crossing (RISC) is critical to ensuring efficient thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF). Herein, a D–A fused core (TCXO) and a D|A–D type emitter (TCXO‐BPA) with a covalently attached auxiliary donor are reported. The TCXO core exhibits high photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) and enables multiple RISC channels from high‐lying triplet states (T n , n ≥ 1) to S 1 with enhanced spin–orbit coupling (SOC). The green organic light‐emitting diode (OLED) based on TCXO achieves a maximum external quantum efficiency (EQE) of 13.4%. Attaching auxiliary donor induces red‐shifted emission and allows precise modulation of RISC kinetics. TCXO‐BPA, featuring a small twist angle, achieves a smaller Δ E ST and a faster RISC rate as high as 1.73 × 10 7 s −1 . The resulting yellow OLED achieves a maximum EQE of 25.3% and a remarkably retained EQE of 14.3% at 1000 cd m −2 . This work demonstrates that D–A fused architecture combined with conformational engineering of auxiliary donor provides an effective strategy for high‐performance TADF‐based OLEDs.

### 13. [Fully Bio-Based Self-Crosslinked Poly(β-myrcene- co -itaconic anhydride) Microspheres Prepared by Self-Stabilized Precipitation Polymerization for Wood Adhesives](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.6c08630)

**Venue/category:** ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering

**DOI:** `10.1021/acssuschemeng.6c08630`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7

**Abstract**

Abstract The continuous deepening of the sustainable development concept has increased the demand for bio-based wood adhesives. Herein, we propose self-crosslinked β-myrcene (MYRC)-itaconic anhydride (ITA) copolymer microspheres prepared via self-stabilized precipitation polymerization (2SP) with fully bio-based structural units for wood adhesive applications. The effects of the solvent, monomer ratio, monomer concentration, initiator concentration, and temperature on the copolymerization behavior and product properties were systematically investigated. Experimental results showed the production of copolymer microspheres with a high yield (up to 67%), micron-scale particle size (2.36 μm), low dispersity (PSD = 1.01), and favorable morphology. The copolymer microspheres were then converted into a wood adhesive through amic acid formation in aqueous solution (which can be derived from green ammonia) and subsequent imidization. The adhesive exhibited high lap-shear strength on wood (2.0 MPa), and great resistance to low temperatures, water, and a wide range of solvents. Given its excellent performance and adherence to the sustainability concept, this adhesive is a promising candidate for sustainable wood-bonding applications.

**Source(s):** Crossref

**Abstract**

Despite the widespread use of gelatin (Gel) in wound dressings due to its biocompatibility, biodegradability, and intrinsic bioactivity, it remains challenging to simultaneously achieve robust mechanical properties and multiple biofunctions in a single hydrogel system. Herein, we constructed a Gel/oxidized chondroitin sulfate (OCS) dynamic Schiff-base network and further introduced triglycidylamine (TGA) as a covalent crosslinker, yielding a Gel/OCS/TGA hydrogel with a proposed dynamic/covalent dual-crosslinked structure. Curcumin liposomes (Cur@Lipo), prepared using microfluidic technology, were then incorporated as bioactive nanofillers to obtain the multifunctional Gel/OCS/TGA/Cur@Lipo composite hydrogel. The hydrogel exhibited self-healing and injectable behavior, which may be associated with the reversible dynamic bonds present in the network. By adjusting the TGA content, the composite hydrogel achieved a tensile strength of 315 kPa, an elongation at break of 258%, and a compressive stress of 72 kPa at 60% strain. Moreover, the hydrogel exhibited sustained curcumin release, marked antioxidant activity (DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging rates of 59.8% and 83.98%, respectively), and antibacterial activity against E. coli and S. aureus (>99.99%), while maintaining good cytocompatibility. In summary, Gel/OCS/TGA/Cur@Lipo multifunctional composite hydrogel is a promising candidate for wound dressing applications, although further in vivo studies are required to confirm its clinical applicability.

### 16. [Chloride‐Mediated Activation of Aluminum‐Alloyed Liquid Metal Anodes for Stretchable Asymmetric Energy Storage](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71752)

**Authors:** Adit Gupta, Yufei Zhang, Jae Uk Choi, Pooi See Lee  
**Venue/category:** Advanced Energy Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**DOI:** `10.1002/aenm.71752`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** stretchable  

**Abstract**

ABSTRACT Gallium‐based liquid metals are attractive anodes for stretchable energy storage because they operate dendrite‐free, yet their bulk faradaic capacity remains locked behind a native oxide shell that conventional electrolytes cannot remove. Here, this passivation problem is resolved through the co‐design of an aluminum‐alloyed gallium–indium (AlGaIn) anode and a chloride‐rich water‐in‐ionic‐liquid (WIL) electrolyte, and the indium‐assisted electrochemical activation mechanism connecting them is identified. Al alloying converts the native oxide into a hydroxyl‐rich mixed Al–Ga–O shell susceptible to [GaCl 4 ] − ‐mediated etching, while the In(III) ions preferentially reduced into metallic patches that seed reversible Ga alloying/dealloying within the WIL. Potential‐resolved ex situ XPS indicates that in conventional TFSI‐based electrolytes, the electrode passivates beneath a TFSI‐derived interphase, whereas in the WIL, a chloride‐dominated electrode–electrolyte interphase forms instead. The activated anode gains 60% capacity during activation and retains ∼100% capacity over 1600 cycles at 500 mA g − 1 . Paired with a sonochemically derived GaOOH nanosheet cathode in a stretchable asymmetric cell, the asymmetric full cell delivers a maximum of 66.28 mWh cm −3 normalized to the active‐electrode volume, and 90% capacity retention over 4000 cycles. The halide‐mediated oxide‐displacement principle established here provides a generalizable electrolyte‐design strategy for oxide‐passivated liquid‐metal and post‐lithium anodes.

### 17. [Solvent-Based Cold-Mix Asphalt for Pavement Pothole Repair: Mechanisms, Performance, Challenges, and Future Perspectives](https://doi.org/10.1061/jpeodx.pveng-2113)

**Authors:** Ran Zhang, Kailu Wang, Long Liang, Jie Ji, Tansen Gao  
**Venue/category:** Journal of Transportation Engineering, Part B: Pavements  
**Date:** 2026-12-01  
**DOI:** `10.1061/jpeodx.pveng-2113`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5  
**Matched:** cross-link  

**Abstract**

Abstract Solvent-based asphalt cold patching materials, commonly used for pothole repair, offer advantages such as ease of application, low carbon emissions, cost-effectiveness, and year-round applicability. However, they face issues such as environmental pollution from volatile solvent emissions, long curing times, and limited durability. This review focuses on the mechanisms of strength development, performance evaluation, and improvement strategies for solvent-based cold patching materials. First, the composition and gradation characteristics of various solvent-based cold patching materials are reviewed. Subsequently, the strength formation mechanism is examined within the Mohr–Coulomb theoretical framework from multiple perspectives, including intermolecular interactions, chemical bonding, and interfacial adhesion theories, such as mechanical interlocking and surface energy theory. A comprehensive assessment of their mechanical properties and road performance also is provided. Finally, strategies for optimizing the performance of solvent-based cold patching materials are discussed, together with an evaluation of their cost-benefit performance and environmental impacts. The analysis demonstrates that the volatile organic diluent (e.g., diesel or biodiesel), as a key component, directly affects performance characteristics, including strength, moisture resistance, and storage stability, through its volatility, solubility parameters, and residual content. The introduction of chemical structures or reactions involving modifiers and additives leads to the formation of peaks, such as carbon–carbon double bonds, ester groups, and carboxylic acids. These changes alter the polarity, cross-linking density, and interfacial activity of diluted asphalt, thereby enhancing the strength, thermal performance across high and low temperatures, and moisture resistance of cold patching materials. Despite these improvements, long-term service performance remains a challenge in the industry, with most research focusing on incorporating modifiers [such as styrene-butadiene-styrene (SBS) polymers, polyurethane, and waste rubber powder] and fillers (for example, cement, fibers, and epoxy resin) into diluted asphalt. A multiperformance optimization model is needed for the synergistic design of material and performance, and further studies should clarify material degradation mechanisms and diluent migration under different environments.
