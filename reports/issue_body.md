**Abstract**

Dye effluents heavily pollute water bodies, presenting severe threats to public health and ecosystems. To address this, a novel chitosan–silica composite was synthesized using waste fish scales and rice husk ash, then evaluated for its efficiency in removing methylene blue (MB) dye from wastewater. The process successfully cross-linked the chitosan and sodium silicate, as confirmed by XRD, Raman, XRF, and FT-IR spectroscopy. Integrating the inorganic silica support enhanced the composite’s mechanical stability and introduced additional binding sites compared to pure chitosan. Batch adsorption studies optimized the removal conditions at a pH of 10, 0.4 g adsorbent dosage, 120-minute contact time, and 50 mg/L initial dye concentration. The equilibrium data adhered closely to the Langmuir isotherm model (R2 = 0.939), yielding a maximum monolayer adsorption capacity (Qmax) of 9.217 mg/g and a Langmuir constant ((KL) of 0.154 L/mg. These results demonstrate that MB adsorption occurs as a monolayer on homogeneous, deprotonated silanol sites. Ultimately, this study highlights the viability of recycling fish scales and rice husk ash into effective, sustainable adsorbents for industrial wastewater treatment.
