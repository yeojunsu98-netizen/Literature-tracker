### 1. [NHC-Modulated Fe (II) Complex for TADF-Assisted Multiphoton Lifetime Imaging: Concurrent Apoptosis Monitoring and Quantification](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11188982/v1)

**Authors:** Qiong Zhang, Yaqin Li, Xiaowei Gong, Yaxiong Wei, Zhou Lu, Xuegang Chen, Hongping Zhou  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11188982/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf  

**Abstract**

Abstract The application of luminescent iron complexes in bioimaging is severely constrained by ultrafast excited-state deactivation and cellular background interference. Herein, we present [Fe(imp)2]2+, the first Fe (II) complex that enables the emergence of both three-photon excited fluorescence (3PEF) and thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF), accessed via stepwise substitution of terpyridine with N‑heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands to circumvent the intrinsic drawbacks of traditional Fe‑based materials. Mechanistically, the enhanced ligand field reshapes the relative energetics of the metal-to-ligand charge transfer (MLCT) and metal-centered (MC) state manifolds, disfavoring MC-mediated nonradiative decay and opening a thermally activated emissive pathway. More significantly, [Fe(imp)2]2+ serves as an apoptosis-related spatiotemporal cascade-targeting agent, sequentially targeting the plasma membrane, mitochondria, and the entire cell. The TADF-assisted lifetime-resolved signatures render [Fe(imp)2]2+ capable of distinguishing apoptosis from mixed cell death, with a global lifetime evolution spanning 1.1-2.4 ns and a pronounced lifetime heterogeneity of 0.43 ns. Co-incubation of [Fe(imp)2]2+ with anti-apoptotic agents demonstrated that effective cytoprotection is achieved when the probe lifetime dropped below 1.2 ns. This work pioneers NHC-regulated Fe (II) complexes TADF-mediated lifetime probes, offering a new paradigm for transition-metal bioimaging.

### 4. [Structure–Property Relationship of Citric Acid-Crosslinked Sericin/PVA Electrospun Nanofibers for Sustainable Dye Adsorption](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12221-026-01599-y)

**Authors:** Jiyun Paik, Sunyoung Yoon, Juhee Yoon, Hyemin Kim, Dongho Shin, Seungoh Jung, Hyoung-Joon Jin, Hyo Won Kwak  
**Venue/category:** Fibers and Polymers  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.1007/s12221-026-01599-y`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** crosslink  

**Abstract**

Abstract In this study, eco-friendly, water stable sericin/poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA) nanofibrous mats were successfully fabricated via aqueous electrospinning, utilizing citric acid (CA) as a green crosslinking agent. The electrospinning performance was optimized by systematically evaluating the shear viscosity of PVA/sericin spinning solutions, achieving the optimal solution viscosity and molecular chain entanglement required for uniform, defect free fiber formation. Subsequent thermal curing with 10 wt% CA established a robust three-dimensional polymer network primarily through esterification between the carboxyl groups of CA and the hydroxyl groups of PVA and sericin, which effectively stabilized the highly porous, interconnected fibrous architecture against dissolution in aqueous environments. Comparative studies using control films supported that sericin intrinsically possesses a high affinity for cationic dyes, while CA serves a crucial dual role: reinforcing the structural matrix and providing abundant unreacted carboxylic acid groups as key adsorption sites. The optimized crosslinked nanofibrous mat exhibited a remarkable maximum methylene blue adsorption capacity of 75.86 mg/g. The adsorption equilibrium and kinetic data were better described by the Langmuir and pseudo-second-order models, respectively, while pH dependent adsorption and pH pzc measurements supported an important contribution of electrostatic interactions. Furthermore, the adsorbent demonstrated outstanding structural stability and reusability, maintaining over 81% of its original dye removal efficiency across ten consecutive adsorption–desorption cycles. By leveraging biodegradable and nontoxic materials, these highly recyclable electrospun nanofibers provide a sustainable, resource efficient platform for advanced wastewater remediation, contributing significantly to the advancement of material reuse and regeneration.

**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 7  
**Matched:** cross-link  

**Abstract**

Abstract We have designed a cross-linker with a more reactive diazoamide attached to one end and a less reactive diazoacetate attached to the other end. In normal solvent acetonitrile, the more reactive diazoamide portion of the cross-linker reacts readily with one carboxylic acid residue. Subsequent buffer exchange into a solvent system containing 1,1,1,3,3,3-hexafluoro-2-propanol (HFIP) promotes the reaction between the less reactive diazoacetate portion of the cross-linker and a second carboxylic acid. This method can be used to join two different carboxylic acid-containing molecules, such as two different peptides, a peptide and a small-molecule carboxylic acid, or several peptides and a protein.

### 6. [Understanding Slide-Ring Gels via Computer Simulation: Formation, Topology, and Mechanical Properties](https://doi.org/10.1021/polymscitech.6c00125)

**Authors:** Bingyi Chen, Yuqi Guo, Yan Wang, Guojie Zhang, Hong Liu, Yingxiang Li  
**Venue/category:** Polymer Science & Technology  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/polymscitech.6c00125`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5  
**Matched:** cross-link  

**Abstract**

Abstract Slide-ring gels are innovative materials characterized by their unique mechanical interlocking structures, where sliding rings, acting also as cross-linking points, are threaded onto a backbone chain, forming a dynamic and adaptable network with distinctive mechanical behaviors. The origin of these specific behaviors is attributed to the unique network structure (the slidable cross-linking points in the network), which is further dependent on the reaction conditions that generate it. In this study, we systematically investigated the reaction kinetics of the slide-ring gels, as well as their topological structures and mechanical properties. Results suggest that the reaction kinetics of slide-ring gels in the early stage can be well described by the second-order reaction equationμ(t)=1−1k′t+1. In the late stage, however, the reaction slows down due to geometrical constraints, reduced chain mobility, and steric shielding that progressively trap the remaining reactive sites. Additionally, the reaction conditions significantly determine whether connections are random or exhibit an “induction effect”. Systems with the “induction effect” show an increase in dimer formation. Furthermore, by examining the material strength, we perform an in-depth characterization of the dynamic behavior of components during the tensile process, which is essentially governed by the network structure. This study helps to reveal the relationship between the reaction kinetics and structural performance of slide-ring gels, and further provides valuable guidance for the optimization and design of high-performance materials.

### 7. [POM-Based Composites with Enhanced Mechanical Strength and UV-Induced Regional Stiffening](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03183)

**Venue/category:** ACS Applied Polymer Materials  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/acsapm.6c03183`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5

**Abstract**

Abstract Organic–inorganic composites have attracted considerable interest for their ability to combine various properties, yet achieving robust and tunable mechanical properties remains challenging. Herein, we incorporate silicotungstic acid (STA), a polyoxometalate with abundant three-dimensional hydrogen-bonding sites and intrinsic photochromic functionality, as a dynamic hydrogen-bond cross-linker into polymer networks, proposing a strategy that enhances mechanical properties of the composite. A polyacrylamide (PAAm)-STA composite was synthesized via a simple one-pot synthesis, and the composite films PAS2 exhibited a tensile strength of 130.0 ± 2.8 MPa, representing a 4.8-fold enhancement over pristine PAAm (28.5 ± 1.4 MPa), while maintaining an average optical transmittance exceeding 90%. To balance strength and toughness, ethylene glycol (EG) was incorporated into the synthesis process at varying concentrations, resulting in a series of composite films with tunable elongation (4–920%) and tensile strength (∼3.4–130.0 MPa). Upon ultraviolet-induced photochromic transition, the transmittance of the PAS2 film can be tuned to as low as 1.7%, the tensile strength can increase to 150.3 ± 2 MPa, and the toughness rises from 4.03 ± 0.11 to 5.18 ± 0.05 MJ/m3. By applying a photomask, region-specific mechanical reinforcement was readily achieved. The composite can be conveniently recycled by dissolving in water, and the reprocessed film retains its outstanding mechanical and optical properties. This strategy of synergistic reinforcement was successfully extended to poly(vinyl alcohol), poly(vinylpyrrolidone), and commercially available 3D-printable polylactic acid resin, confirming its broadly applicable. The present approach not only improves the overall mechanical performance of the material but also imparts postfabrication tunability, offering insights into the design of adaptive functional materials.

### 8. [Harnessing Surface Wrinkling Instability for Visualizing Dynamic Chemistry in Polymer Networks](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c17944)

**Authors:** Mengling Yang, Shuzhen Yan, Yuchan Liao, Wenbin Wang, Xueliang Jiang, Xuesong Jiang, Xuzhou Yan  
**Venue/category:** Journal of the American Chemical Society  
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**DOI:** `10.1021/jacs.6c17944`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 5  
**Matched:** cross-link  

**Abstract**

Abstract Studying dynamic chemical behaviors is fundamental to understanding and regulating the dynamic responses and functions of polymer materials. However, real-time, intuitive, and selective visualization of these molecular processes─particularly when multiple dynamic motifs coexist─remains a major challenge. Herein, we introduce surface wrinkles as a visualization platform that directly translates dynamic chemical behaviors in polymers into observable macroscopic morphological changes. Using a dual-cross-linked polymer network (DCN) containing quadruple hydrogen bonds and mechanical bonds as a model system, we fabricate stable wrinkles via modulus mismatch in a bilayer architecture. In situ microscopy reveals that acid-triggered dissociation of quadruple hydrogen bonds causes rapid wrinkle erasure due to network disruption, whereas base-induced rotaxane sliding leads to gradual wrinkle erasure owing to network relaxation, reflecting their distinct dynamic mechanisms and time scales. Rheological analyses on single-cross-linked model networks corroborate these macroscopic observations at the molecular level. Moreover, by programming wrinkle patterns with “write–hide–read” functionality, we achieve macroscopic visualization and differentiation of microscopic dynamic processes. This work establishes surface wrinkling as a powerful and intuitive platform for real-time monitoring of dynamic chemistry and provides valuable insights for designing functional polymer materials with tunable macroscopic properties.
