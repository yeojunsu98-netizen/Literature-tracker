### 1. [The film, not the molecule, decides how deep-blue TADF hosts rank on gap and thermal robustness](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011186/v1)

**Authors:** Manmohan Dash, M. N. Satyanarayan, Chaithanya Purushottam Bhat, Debashis Bandyopadhyay  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15011186/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf  

**Abstract**

Deep-blue host materials for thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) are usually ranked on a few molecular numbers, on the assumption that the isolated molecule anticipates the film. We test that assumption for three phosphine-oxide hosts, S1, S3 and S4, by reading protocol-locked descriptors of the dilute solution, the evaporated film, the powder and the bulk side by side, each with its uncertainty, next to a separate density-functional layer. The descriptors fall into families that rank the hosts differently: film-gap and thermal descriptors place the multi-arm S4 host first, emission and order descriptors place the compact carbazole S1 host first, and band-tail and voltammetric descriptors do not separate the two. The split appears between the molecule and the film: the two hosts emit alike as dilute molecules, yet the S4 film absorption edge lies 0.33 eV higher. Calculations validated against the measured infrared and NMR spectra show frontier levels that move far more with the functional than between hosts. Film gap and thermal robustness must therefore be measured and assessed separately from emissive quality.

### 2. [Choosing a DFT Protocol That Ranks Deep-Blue TADF Emitters and Pairs Them with Hosts](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011190/v1)

**Authors:** Manmohan Dash, M. N. Satyanarayan, Mallesham Godumala, Jayathirtha Rao Vaidya  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.26434/chemrxiv.15011190/v1`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 13  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf  

**Abstract**

Computational screening of deep-blue thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials decides which emitters are made and which host each is paired with, yet the descriptors behind these decisions are rarely tested against measurement. We test them on 50 molecules (39 published emitters, eight references and three phosphine-oxide hosts) against measured data for 27 emitters. The range-separated ωB97X-D3 functional overestimates every singlettriplet gap (∆EST) and fails to rank the emitters because its error differs between molecular families; a family offset restores the ranking, and the global hybrids PBE0 and B3LYP rank the gaps directly (Spearman ρ = 0.59 and 0.58). Rate constants built on computed gaps are unreliable, but become accurate once the measured gap is used. For host–emitter pairing, the triplet energies of host and emitter must be computed on one footing: a calibrated host combined with as-computed emitters misclassifies almost every confined pair. A tiered, costed protocol turns these findings into selection rules for screening campaigns.

### 3. [Concentration quenching resistant violet-blue TADF OLEDs via indirect conformal distributions tuning in solid-state](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78909-8)

**Authors:** Hong-Ji Tan, Guo-Xi Yang, Jie-Rong Yu, Zhi-Qiang Long, Qing-Xiao Tong, Ze-Lin Zhu, Yin-Xuan Zhang, Daniel M. Dawson, Sharon E. Ashbrook, Shi-Jian Su, Jing-Xin Jian, Eli Zysman-Colman et al.  
**Venue/category:** Nature Communications  
**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41467-026-78909-8`  
**Source(s):** Crossref  
**Keyword score:** 11  
**Matched:** tadf, oled  

**Abstract**

Abstract Controlling conformation distributions is the key to manipulate material properties of multi-conformal molecules. Distinct from previous strategy with direct change or impact on the flexible groups, we tune the conformation distribution through changing peripheral groups to influence their intermolecular interactions. Two blue violet TADF emitters, namely tert -butyl substituted 5-(2,12-di-tert-butyl-5,9-dioxa-13b-boranaphtho[3,2,1-de]anthracen-7-yl)−10,10-diphenyl-5,10-dihydrodibenzo[b,e][1,4]azagermine (TDBA-PAG) and cumenyl substituted 5-(2,12-bis(2-phenylpropan-2-yl)−5,9-dioxa-13b-boranaphtho[3,2,1-de]anthracen-7-yl)−10,10-diphenyl-5,10-dihydrodibenzo[b,e][1,4]azagermine (TPDBA-PAG) were designed and synthesized. With the same germanium-integrated donor, the two compounds show similar emission properties in solution with calculated conformation dominantly of their quasi-axial (QA) forms (> 97%). However, TPDBA-PAG presents a lower QA ratio compared with TDBA-PAG, which endows the former with more pronounced TADF emission, higher emission efficiency and reduced concentration quenching phenomena in solid state. As a result, TPDBA-PAG performed as one of the best non-doped and doped violet-blue emitters and successfully sensitized MesB-DIDOBNA-N to achieve record high EQE max of 24.7% at 406 nm, making significant progress in NUV OLEDs.
